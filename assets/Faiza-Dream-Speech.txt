Faiza Baloch – Dream Speech
Women in AI Impact Award · Chicago · December 12, 2051

Good evening, everyone. Wow. Thank you so much.

The Women in AI Impact Award… I still can’t believe I’m the one standing up here.

Let me ask you something. What is easy in life? Nothing. Everything we get, we work hard for. Everybody is struggling with something. A student studying late at night. A father working day and night so his kids have food, school, and a doctor. Even a little bird flies miles and miles to bring food back to her babies. Life is hard. But still, this world is beautiful.

My story starts with my father. He was working in Oman, so I was born there, under the golden sun of the Middle East. When I was 12, we moved to Germany. New country, new language, new religion, new culture. I didn’t understand anything. And German? For me, it was the hardest language in the world.

At 18, I went to nursing school. Thousands of medical words, medicines, formulas, and 12-hour shifts taking care of patients. And a couple years later COVID came. Fun times, right? We worked 14, 15, 16 hours a day, scared for our patients, scared of ourselves, scared for our families.

Behind our masks, we hid our tears,
We held the hands of strangers through their fears.
When the world went quiet and the nights were long,
Love kept us going, and love kept us strong.

And my dad wasn’t with us. He was still in the Middle East, still working for us.

Later, he moved to the United States, our whole family followed. Again, a new language, a new home, a new life. Starting from zero. Then a friend asked me, “Faiza, have you heard about i.c.stars?”

That question changed my life. I went from nursing to IT and AI. Our very first project was building an AI model. I had never done anything like that. i.c.stars, thank you. You gave me a chance to learn, to grow, and to dream again.

From there, I worked in IT support, then AI development, building my own apps, then AI security and safety, because I saw how important it is to protect people and their data. Today, I’m the Director of AI Safety at i.c.stars, and I co-founded my company, Radar, to make AI safe and fair for everyone.

But tonight’s award is not really about my job. It’s about why I did all of this.

Half of the people in this world are women. There is a saying from the Ghanaian educator James Aggrey: “If you educate a man, you educate an individual. But if you educate a woman, you educate a family.” I have seen this with my own eyes. When a woman learns, her children learn. Her family grows. Her community grows.

And I know what it feels like to start from zero, more than once. So I made a promise: no woman should be left behind in this AI era.

It started small, with free weekend workshops at libraries and community centers. I taught women that AI is not scary. It’s a tool to help with their jobs, their businesses, and their families. And I taught them how to protect themselves from AI scams, deepfakes, and people stealing their data.

Then those women started coming back as mentors for the next group. So I built a full program and called it AI Impact, because I wanted every woman to make an impact with AI. And tonight, standing here with the Women in AI Impact Award, it’s a program that any organization could run. Today it’s in use in thousands of cities and 80 countries, and millions of women have gone through it. Some found new jobs in tech. Some started their own companies. Some are teaching it now themselves.

I believed in myself. Now I want every woman to believe in herself too, to be independent, skilled, and confident in this robot era.

I didn’t do this alone. To my parents: you gave up so much so I could have more. Everything I am is because of you. To i.c.stars, Christeno, Larry, Brandies, the whole team, and my cycle: you believed in me before I believed in myself. And to my guests tonight, thank you for every step.

I was born beneath Oman’s golden sun,
In Germany, my long road has just begun.
Now America is the place I call home,
and on every step, I never walked alone.

My journey is not over. I’ll keep learning, keep sharing, and keep helping women in tech. And to every woman here tonight: if I can do it, you can do it too.

Thank you so much. Have a great night!
